# Analysis protocol

The target is the annual global count of earthquakes with preferred USGS magnitude at least 7.0. Count events by UTC calendar year. The 1918–2020 input is a revised USGS ComCat snapshot with mixed magnitude types. This is an exploratory retrospective evaluation after an initial examination of the historical series.

Evaluate expanding training windows at 36 common forecast origins (1977–2012) and horizons one through eight. Fit every model on the same conditional responses, from the second observation through the origin. The initial comparison fits independent Poisson, independent negative binomial (NB2), independent two-Poisson mixture, Poisson autoregression, and a two-component Poisson autoregressive mixture with constant weights. The autoregressions use log mean equal to an intercept plus a slope times the log of one plus the previous count. Constrain each autoregressive slope to [−0.95, 0.95]. Use deterministic multistart likelihood fitting with exact simpler-model candidates and keep start diagnostics.

Compute plug-in count distributions with finite-state transition recursion. Choose the state cap using training information and numerical checks. Normalize retained path mass, record the escaped mass, and check first moments, a reference ranked probability score (RPS), and quantiles after doubling the cap. Check numerical error in the observed log score separately. Prediction intervals condition on fitted parameters.

Use negative log predictive probability (NLL) as the primary loss. Also report RPS, root mean squared error of forecast means, mean absolute error of discrete predictive medians, and coverage and width for inclusive equal-tailed 80% and 95% intervals. Report each horizon, pool with equal weights per origin–horizon case, and split the origins into the first and last 18. The forecast cases overlap. Report descriptive differences.

The 2021–2025 supplement scores only the five initial models. Generate the 2020-origin forecast distributions using data through 2020, freeze the distributions, then score against the five annual outcomes. The forecasts were computed retrospectively in 2026.

After the historical and supplementary outcomes were known, the exploratory extension added NB2 autoregression at the same historical origins and horizons. Use an intercept in [−8, 8], a slope in [−0.95, 0.95], and positive dispersion in [1e−8, 10]. Use 12 starts from slopes −0.5, 0, and 0.5 crossed with dispersions 0.02, 0.1, 0.3, and 1. Include the exact Poisson AR and independent NB2 cases. Compare one-year mixtures of the six forecast distributions with equal weights or with weights updated by the previous realized outcomes. Begin with equal weights and update once per observed year. Check the standard cumulative log-loss identity for likelihood weighting and the bound of log(6) above the best fixed expert's cumulative loss.

## Time-series strengthening protocol, 11 November 2026

The following protocol fixes the inputs, comparisons, and diagnostic calculations. The earlier USGS outcomes were known. The extension is exploratory.

Data: preserve both existing USGS snapshots and all v1.1.0 code/results. Primary USGS target is the global UTC annual count with preferred magnitude >=7, years1918–2025, outer origins1977–2024 (48 targets1978–2025). Before any ISC scoring, verify supplied isc-gem.zip version13.0, release21October2026, coverage through2022, documented columns, magnitudes, UTC times and unique IDs. Main ISC target is main-catalogue Mw (direct or proxy) >=7 in1918–2022, with45 targets1978–2022. Use complete annual calendars including zeros. Compare catalogues on these same45 targets; do not splice catalogues or describe their overlapping earthquakes as independent realizations. Preserve the raw archive/hash and CC-BY-SA3.0 attribution; its supplementary catalogue is a separate sensitivity, not silently merged into the main series. Exclude rows with missing/unusable magnitude from threshold counting and report each count; stop for an unresolved schema or identity defect rather than guess.

Fixed training histories contain20,30,40 annual observations or expand from1918; response years start at the second annual observation. Reuse the bounded Gaussian AR, Poisson AR and stable NB2 fitting definitions, starts and nested candidates from ar_reassessment.py. Score the response mean and Gaussian AR under all four histories. For count distributions cross no-lag/lag with Poisson/NB2 under all four histories using exactly matched responses. The independent Poisson mean equals the response mean; score the existing iid-NB2 fit explicitly. Retain the Gaussian mixture under expanding and30-year histories with the existing SD lower bounds0.5,1,2; do not tune mixtures over more histories. Baselines also include persistence and the last10-year mean. All main point forecasts are unrounded conditional means; retain and flag any negative mean rather than clip. Gaussian density values never enter the count probability-score comparison. Use cached fits at identical origins/inputs and retain unsuccessful starts; never omit a forecast case.

Nested history selection: at each outer origin o, use exactly20 preceding realized one-year forecasts, with inner origins o-20,...,o-1 and targets o-19,...,o. Each inner forecast fits through its own origin. Select among {20,30,40,expanding} by the response mean's inner mean squared error. Apply that SAME selected history to the response mean, Gaussian AR and all four count distributions at outer origin o. This keeps the history fixed when estimating added lag or dispersion value. The earliest inner origin1957 has40 observations from1918, so every candidate is available. Ties within1e-10 squared-count units choose the longer history, with expanding longest. Record every candidate inner loss and selected history. There is no performance-driven grid expansion or retrospective best-history selection.

Smooth-mean control: at origin o, assign response years s=1919,...,o weights proportional to 2^[-(o-s)/h] for fixed half-lives h={5,10,20,40,infinity}. Infinity equals the expanding response mean. Score each fixed half-life and a selector using the same20 prior realized squared errors; choose the longer half-life on a1e-10 tie. This tests gradual discounting without an AR coefficient. No new trend, neural, INGARCH, change-point search or simulation is added in this bounded round; these do not directly resolve the leading measurement/history question and would introduce extra choices. Parameter uncertainty is not integrated; do not make improved-interval claims from plug-in fits.

Scores and diagnostics: primary point loss is squared error, summarized as RMSE. Report MAE of the SAME means, mean signed error and per-year errors. Count PMFs use direct integer support, stable NB log probabilities, exact Poisson limit, NLL and RPS, 80%/95% inclusive equal-tailed interval coverage and width. Check finite probabilities, normalization, mean and tail truncation, quantile stability, and identities at zero slope/dispersion. Report a deterministic discrete-PIT histogram by allocating each observation uniformly over its left/right CDF interval and integrating its contributions to10 equal bins. This is an averaged randomized-PIT diagnostic, not10 independent tests or a uniformity p-value. Save raw-series ACF through lag10 and forecast-error ACF through lag4 without significance bands, period means/variances and AR coefficient paths. No diagnostic p-value selects a model.

Paired comparisons: define d_t=loss(method)-loss(reference), so negative values favor the method. Main point contrasts are recent30mean versus expandingmean; recent30GaussianAR versus expandingGaussianAR; recent30GaussianAR versus recent30mean; recent30mixture versus recent30GaussianAR atSDfloor1; selectedmean versus recent30mean and versus expandingmean; selectedGaussianAR versus selectedmean; selecteddiscountedmean versus recent30mean. Count contrasts compare NB versus Poisson holding lag/history fixed and lag versus no-lag holding distribution/history fixed, for expanding,recent30,and selected histories. Report mean loss differences, each RMSE/NLL, wins, cumulative differences, leave-one-target-year-out ranges, and ranges after omitting each contiguous five-target block from scoring without refitting. Preserve all targets in primary scores. Report the existing1978–2000,2001–2020,2021–2025 periods, truncated at2022 for ISC, plus common1978–2022 comparisons.

Approximate uncertainty: for each declared paired contrast calculate Bartlett HAC intervals with lag3 and fixed sensitivities0,1,5. With n paired targets and centered differences, gamma_k=n^-1 sum_{t=k+1}^n(d_t-dbar)(d_{t-k}-dbar); V=[gamma_0+2sum_{k=1}^L(1-k/(L+1))*gamma_k]/n. Interval dbar +/-1.96sqrt(V). Check nonnegative V to floating tolerance and verify by an independent matrix calculation. These pointwise, exploratory intervals assume weak dependence and a stable loss-difference mean; they are not familywise, selection-adjusted, or proof of future performance. Report the sensitivity and cumulative path when this assumption is doubtful. Do not use stars or pursue a preferred rejection.

Locked sensitivities: repeat the principal fixed and selected point/count comparisons separately for USGS >7 and USGS >=7.5 using retained event records, including zero-count years. For ISC repeat main versus main-plus-supplement >=7 with explicit deduplication checks; main-only is primary. Do not pool thresholds as independent evidence. For both main catalogues compare expanding-from1918, expanding-from1964 and fixed30 on targets1994–2022 (29 targets), without re-tuning the selector on an infeasible short record. Show annual catalogue-count differences and magnitude-boundary exclusion counts. If catalogue conclusions diverge, audit documented identifiers and a fixed descriptive match rule: exact ISC identifier where present in USGS source IDs; otherwise mutually unique candidates within120seconds and200km, record ambiguous/unmatched events. This audit describes recorded measurement differences; it cannot assign a cause or force a complete mapping.
